TOK Year 1 (Grade 11) · Lesson plan Anchor: "Knowledge and technology" · Knowledge Framework: Methods and Tools · TOK Concept: Truth
Unit / Anchor text
Knowledge and technology: Can we trust what AI tells us?
Objective SWBAT…
Scholars will be able to apply three truth criteria, does it correspond to the facts, is it internally coherent, and does it work in practice, to judge whether an AI-generated claim can be trusted as knowledge.
Why It Matters
TOK is about judging knowledge claims. AI states things with confidence but does not verify them, so a knower needs clear criteria to decide how far to trust an AI answer.
IB Framework
Knowledge Framework: Methods and Tools (what counts as good evidence?)
TOK Concept: Truth
Conceptual / Knowledge Question: To what extent can we trust knowledge produced by AI?
Learner Profile: Thinkers · Inquirers
ATLs: Thinking (critical) · Research
Key Points
  • Three truth criteria: correspondence (fits the facts), coherence (holds together), pragmatic (works in practice).
  • Generative AI predicts likely text; it does not check whether the text is true.
  • Confident wording is not evidence of truth.
Central Vocabulary / Command Term
Command terms: apply · evaluate · justify. AI AI terms: generative AI · hallucination · claim · verification.
Potential Misunderstandings
  • "If it sounds confident, it is correct." Fluency is not reliability.
  • "AI checks its own facts." It predicts patterns; it does not verify.
Do First · Do Now 5 min
ROUTINE The Year 1 "I know…" opener. AI One claim on the board is copied from an AI chatbot. Is it true? How would you check?
Introduction to New Material & Teacher Model 15 min · I do
AI Concept Video 1 introduces the three truth criteria. The teacher then models applying all three to one AI answer, out loud: does it correspond to the facts, is it coherent, does it work in practice?
Guided Practice 13 min · we do
ROUTINE Their "refute or confirm the claim" structure. AI In pairs, scholars apply the three criteria to two AI outputs and decide, for each, trustworthy or not, naming the criterion that decided it.
Independent Practice 7 min · you do
AI Each scholar applies the three truth criteria to one new AI claim on their own, and writes a verdict with the reason.
Formative Assessment / Exit Ticket 5 min
Given one AI-generated claim, apply the three truth criteria and judge whether it can be trusted, stating which criterion was decisive. Exemplar in the Teacher Guide.
Feeds the IB-graded pieces IB
Exhibition: the criteria transfer directly to the IA prompt "What counts as good evidence for a claim?" Essay: builds source-evaluation habits for reliability-of-knowledge arguments. Scaffolds toward both; replaces neither.

What each of the 18 elements IS, what the expert provides, and what the design team builds. This is for everyone, the partner and the expert alike, so we share one definition of what the map asks for. Examples are from a foundations lesson; the elements are the same for every grade.

The audience everything is written for: IB Diploma Theory of Knowledge juniors (grade 11), about 16 to 17 years old, mixed reading levels with emergent bilingual scholars, 45-minute periods, Chromebooks; the course carries 1 TOK credit. Reading-level rule: scholar-facing prose at grade 7 to 9 readability; every video captioned; academic vocabulary taught with cognates for emergent bilingual scholars; nothing depends on sound or idioms.

Foundation

1 · Learning outcome one observable sentence

What a scholar can do after the lesson, in observable verbs; never "understand" or "know." The expert names the capability; the design team phrases it and aligns the assessment.

2 · Key concepts the 3 to 5 ideas that must survive

If a scholar remembers only these, the lesson worked. Each maps to an assessment item and usually a video. The expert provides the concepts and the misconceptions that fight them. In TOK these become a knowledge question.

3 · Vocabulary, Beck's three tiers EB instruction standard

Tier 1 everyday words · Tier 2 high-utility academic words (the sweet spot for EB scholars, with cognates) · Tier 3 domain terms. Focus on Tiers 2 and 3. In TOK, AI terms sit beside the IB command terms.

4 · Standards mapping the course's governing document

The standard the lesson satisfies. TOK is a Texas-approved innovative course with no TEKS, so alignment is to the IB Theory of Knowledge Guide, which defines the course's expectations, not a TEKS code. Design-team task; the expert provides nothing.

Scholar materials

5 · Student-facing content the lesson body, in chunks

Hook → concept (with video) → guided try → independent try → close. Concrete before abstract; a real-world hook every time. The expert supplies substance and a worked example; AI drafts to the audience; the expert signs off.

6 · Concept videos ×2 60 to 90 sec, one idea each

One short captioned video per key-concept cluster: one idea, one analogy. The videos carry the teaching so the teacher need not be an AI expert.

7 · Scenario video 90 to 120 sec, a real moment

The idea working or failing in a real moment: near-peer character, concrete task, consequence. In TOK it also seeds a knowledge question.

8 · In-class activity do or judge, not watch

Hands-on practice during the period: stump it · catch it · fix it · judge it. The expert says what practice should look like and what failure looks like.

9 · Going-deeper segment one step past the intro

The layer that keeps the period rich. In TOK this is the knowledge-question debate that feeds the Essay and Exhibition. The expert provides the one insight they wish scholars knew.

10 · Extension activity optional, after class

A deeper optional task for scholars who want more; differentiates without depending on homework.

Teacher enablement

11 · Teacher Guide template, 11 sections

Makes the lesson teachable by a non-expert: the one idea, why it matters, prep, run of show, teacher moves, misconceptions, differentiation, materials, assessment + key, connection, open items. The expert provides the misconceptions, the why, and the pitfalls.

12 · Teacher video 2 to 3 min

The guide's elevator version: why it matters, the one idea to protect, the moves to lean on, the activity moment, the misconception to watch, how to read the exit ticket. Watched once before teaching.

Assessment, supports & future

13 · Formative assessment exit ticket

Built to auto-grade where possible; every item maps to a key concept. The expert provides one assessment idea and the misconceptions that become distractors.

14 · Answer key / rubric scoring

Scoring for every item and which misses signal which reteach. Lives in the Teacher Guide.

15 · Pre-assessment / baseline pre/post data

Short baseline before the unit; items mirror the formative banks. Runs in the partner's systems; the university receives de-identified aggregates.

16 · Discussion scaffolds talking points + sentence stems

Teacher talking points + scholar sentence stems, bilingual-friendly, using Tier 2 vocabulary so academic language gets rehearsed.

17 · AI-tutor knowledge base RAG, internal

The content distilled into retrievable chunks, embedded so the future tutor answers grounded in approved content. The expert checks accuracy. Internal until the tutor ships.

18 · Language Support Pack EB support, bundled

One reviewable deliverable bundling the lesson’s language scaffolds for emergent-bilingual scholars: a tiered glossary (Tiers 2 and 3 taught, concept-critical Tier 1 glossed for newcomers, with cognates), sentence frames and stems for every activity and discussion, and bilingual response options for open items. Built from the vocabulary, discussion, and assessment elements; lands in the TOK Accommodations / Modifications menu.
