One spine, three depths. In Year One every scholar gets the same topic arc, starting from the same foundations: grade 10 covers the arc in full, grade 12 covers it in a condensed senior pass, and grade 11 covers the deep first half this year and completes the arc next year in grade 12. What changes by grade is the course frame (CTE capstone in grade 10, TOK in grades 11 and 12), the depth, and the lesson count. Only the grade 12 map changes. Grade 10 and grade 11 stay exactly as reviewed. Tab ② explains why grade 12 is the one piece that must change, and tab ③ shows how the design settles into steady state.

Reading the grid, and who owns what. The four phases are our content arc, not the partner's unit structure. Each course packages the arc its own way: grade 10's course wraps phases 1 and 2 into its Unit 1 (the taught Lessons 1 to 6, which launch the capstone) and then draws phases 3 and 4 into the capstone as a content menu; the TOK courses deliver the phases as lessons with one knowledge question, one best-fit Knowledge Framework part, and one TOK Concept each. We supply finished lessons written in the partner's own lesson-plan format (each grade map's tab ②); where each lesson sits in the partner's units and calendar is the partner's decision, campus by campus.

Same concepts, never the same lesson · worked example

The Year One principle is same topics, not same objectives. Each grade meets the same foundational concept through its own lens, register, and assessment product. Lesson 1 shows the pattern:

L1 · What Is AI, Really?

Grade 10 · CTE lens · practical

Objective: define AI, ML and automation; distinguish narrow from general AI; identify AI in daily life.

Register: hands-on sorting and daily-life examples; ends with a check for understanding; feeds the capstone project.

L1 · What Is AI, Really?

Grade 11 · TOK Year 1 lens · epistemic

Objective: evaluate what separates intelligence from automation, and how confidently we can classify real systems.

TOK frame: tagged Scope · Certainty; ends on the knowledge question; feeds the Exhibition. Already built and delivered from the same source content.

L1 · What Is AI + How Machines Learn

Grade 12 · TOK Year 2 lens · senior, condensed · proposed

Objective: judge what we can responsibly claim about a system's output once we know how it was trained.

TOK frame: proposed Methods and Tools · Evidence; position writing that feeds the Essay; condensed with ML basics for the senior pass.

Same foundational concepts in all three columns, and no two grades share an objective sentence, a knowledge question, or an assessment product. This is the program's demonstrated adaptation method: the grade 11 version of this exact lesson was produced from the grade 10 source content and delivered for review, with a documented trace of what changed and why.

The grant frame · hours and the mastery target

Grade 10 · the ~22-hour rule. The grant reserves about 22 AI contact hours per 10th grader inside the CTE course, filled as a container: the taught lessons (about 4.5 to 9 hours), plus the capstone project and case studies filling the balance. The 45-minute model keeps the taught portion lean so the project fits; teaching all 14 titles as required 90-minute lectures would crowd the project out, which is exactly what the pacing feedback warned against.
Grades 11 and 12 · Year One exceeds the promised bridge. For this first year the grant promised a lighter one-time foundations supplement of about 2 hours inside TOK. The structure on this page delivers about 6 hours in grade 12 and 6 to 12 in grade 11: more than the promise, not less. The partner's own scheduling constraints (about three weeks in grade 11, about two in grade 12) already assume more than 2 hours. The grant team confirms this reading before the TOK maps change.
The mastery target needs grade 12 to have foundations. The grant's headline literacy target measures mastery for 10th and 12th graders on shared pre and post assessments. A Year One senior course without foundations would be assessed on constructs it never taught. Same topics for grade 12 is what makes the target measurable.

Step 1 · June

The program's own drafts gave every grade the same topics

The per-grade curriculum documents drafted in June already matched the position now on the table: grade 11 was 16 lessons across four units, beginning with Foundations of AI, and grade 12 was a standalone condensed 8-lesson pass whose Unit 1 is "AI Foundations (Condensed)". Same arc, three depths. Grade 12 was designed to stand on its own, not to depend on a prior year.

Step 2 · July

The expert TOK review re-cut the two years as one sequence

During the TOK facilitator's review of the draft maps, the two TOK years were restructured as one continuous sequence for one student: Year 1 carries the first half of the content, Year 2 carries the second half, so a student who takes both years never repeats a topic. Sixteen lessons were also judged too many to carry inside TOK, so each year was capped at eight. This fixed a real problem in the drafts (for that continuing student, the old Year 2 largely repeated Year 1), and it is sound design for a student who flows through both years.

Step 3 · The hidden assumption

The continuation model assumes every senior already had Year 1

That is true from Year Two of the program onward. It is not true in Year One, when the seniors have had no prior AI coursework at all. Under the current maps, Year One seniors would meet accountability, deepfakes, and AI in industry without ever having learned what AI is, how it learns, or how it fails. The grant anticipated exactly this gap: it planned a one-time foundations bridge for grades 11 and 12 in the first year, precisely because those students never got the earlier introduction.

Bottom line. This is not a disagreement between two curricula; it is two time frames. The continuation split is right for every student with two TOK years ahead, this year's juniors included. It fails only for this year's seniors, the one cohort that would leave the program with no AI foundations. The fix is surgical: give Year One's grade 12 the condensed full arc, and leave everything else exactly as reviewed.

③ How both designs fit together over time

What stays from the reviewed maps

  • The grade 10 map, in full. Locked curriculum, 45/90 model, capstone, standards tab; untouched by this decision.
  • The grade 11 map, in full. Its structure stays exactly as the TOK review shaped it; this cohort completes the arc in grade 12 next year, and the built grade 11 Lesson 1 stays valid as delivered.
  • The eight lessons on the grade 12 map. They keep their design and tagging and remain the steady-state grade 12 curriculum from Year Two on; only Year One's seniors get the condensed pass instead.
  • The whole TOK treatment from the facilitator's review: the TOK lesson-plan format, one objective per lesson, one best-fit Knowledge Framework part plus one TOK Concept, single 45-minute periods, alignment to the IB TOK Guide, and the academic-integrity guardrails.
  • The 18-element spine and the teacher package structure, identical for every grade.
  • The steady-state continuation itself. The facilitator's two-year design is not discarded; it becomes the labeled plan for Year Two onward.

What would change

  • The grade 12 curriculum tab shows the condensed full arc for Year One (foundations included) instead of Units 3 and 4 only.
  • The grade 12 map gains a Year One vs steady state distinction, so the reviewed continuation stays visible as the destination rather than reading as this year's plan. No other map changes.

What program leadership is deciding

  1. Confirm the Year One shape: grade 12 gets the condensed full arc (foundations included); grade 11 stays as published and completes the arc in grade 12 next year; grade 10 unchanged.
  2. Grade 11's length: resolved. It keeps its 8 published lessons; the TOK review's cap and structure stand, and no new grade 11 design work is needed.
  3. Reconcile with the grant plan: Year One grade 12 coverage exceeds the promised two-hour bridge rather than breaking it; the grant team should confirm that reading.
  4. The grade 12 map is updated to this structure (v0.9, live) and shows Year One and steady state side by side; the partner and the TOK facilitator review it there.
